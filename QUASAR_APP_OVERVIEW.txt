QUASAR — PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What QUASAR solves
------------------
QUASAR is a route-planning and delivery-operations application for last-mile logistics teams. It helps dispatchers assign delivery stops to vehicles, create capacity-aware routes, visualise the plan on real OpenStreetMap geography, and keep an auditable record of each optimization run.

The immediate problem is practical: late deliveries, unnecessary mileage, uneven vehicle loading, and dispatcher decisions that are difficult to explain after the fact. QUASAR turns a list of delivery locations, package demand, and vehicle capacities into a validated route plan with traceable optimization evidence.

How the application works
-------------------------
1. A dispatcher selects a local scenario, imports orders, or adds delivery stops on the map.
2. The dispatcher configures the fleet: vehicle names and capacity limits.
3. QUASAR sends the request to a FastAPI backend.
4. The backend builds a distance matrix from road-network data when available. If the road graph is unavailable, it safely records and uses a Haversine great-circle fallback instead of silently mixing distance metrics.
5. Google OR-Tools solves the production vehicle-routing problem (CVRP) for multi-vehicle delivery, enforcing capacity constraints.
6. The API stores the input, solver results, distance basis, fleet allocation, and quantum job trace in SQLite.
7. The React dashboard presents the route plan, individual vehicle routes, operational estimates, benchmark results, and a selectable history of completed runs.

Quantum component and honest scope
---------------------------------
QUASAR is a hybrid system, not a claim that a quantum computer already replaces classical fleet routing.

For a small, single-vehicle problem of up to three stops, QUASAR can run a cost-Hamiltonian QAOA path on a local simulator or an IBM Quantum backend when configured. The application records the actual returned job IDs and backend name.

For fleet scenarios, OR-Tools remains the production solver. QUASAR can also evaluate a bounded QAOA+ XY-mixer warm-start experiment on a small neighbourhood around the classical route. This is research evidence and does not replace the fleet allocation. The UI labels that distinction explicitly.

This architecture is intentional: classical optimization scales the operational workflow today, while the pluggable quantum path allows the team to benchmark and adopt improved hardware or algorithms when they are independently validated.

Operational outputs
-------------------
- Capacity-feasible vehicle assignments and delivery order.
- Solver objective and the recorded distance metric.
- OSRM road-geometry map preview, clearly separated from the solver objective when they differ.
- Estimated travel time, fuel use, and fuel-only cost. These are assumption-based estimates, not unproven savings claims.
- Per-vehicle route selection and route highlighting.
- Benchmark history with original solver rows and quantum job traces for each saved run.

Target customers
----------------
- Small and medium logistics operators.
- Local retailers and e-commerce sellers with their own delivery fleet.
- Agricultural cooperatives moving products from collection points to a local depot.
- Municipal, relief, or social-good delivery programs that need transparent routing decisions.

Business model
--------------
1. Software-as-a-Service subscription
   - Starter: one depot, a small fleet, route planning, map view, and run history.
   - Professional: multiple depots, CSV/API import, operational analytics, team accounts, and higher run limits.
   - Enterprise: private deployment, SSO, custom integrations, audit retention, and service-level support.

2. Usage-based optimization credits
   Customers can pay for optimization volume above the subscription allowance, for example by route run, vehicle, or monthly delivery-stop volume.

3. Integration and implementation services
   Revenue can come from onboarding, connecting order-management systems, fleet telematics, warehouse software, or a customer-specific emissions model.

4. Future quantum-ready research tier
   Advanced customers or research partners may pay for controlled quantum benchmarking, hardware-job orchestration, result caching, and technical reports. This tier must never promise a quantum speedup without measured evidence.

Why QUASAR is differentiated
-----------------------------
QUASAR combines operational usefulness with scientific transparency. It provides a classical production route plan now, preserves the evidence needed to audit it, and exposes quantum experiments without overstating their capability. That makes it suitable for both a realistic logistics demo and a responsible pathway toward quantum-ready optimization.
