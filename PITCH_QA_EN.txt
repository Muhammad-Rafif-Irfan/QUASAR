QUASAR — LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

1. What problem does QUASAR solve?
QUASAR helps delivery operators assign stops to vehicles and generate capacity-feasible routes. It reduces manual dispatch work and makes the route decision traceable through stored solver results, distance metrics, and job evidence.

2. Is QUASAR actually using quantum computing?
Yes, but we state the scope precisely. Standard QAOA is implemented for a bounded single-vehicle TSP of up to three stops. QAOA+ is implemented as a bounded local-improvement experiment around a classical CVRP seed. Production multi-vehicle allocation is solved by OR-Tools today.

3. Why do all three solvers sometimes return the same distance?
The benchmark instance can be very small. For three stops, Nearest Neighbor may already find the optimum, so OR-Tools and QAOA find the same valid route. We show this as a tie, not as a quantum advantage.

4. Does QUASAR claim a quantum speedup?
No. Simulator wall-clock time is not a speedup claim. We compare measured route quality, validity, execution evidence, and quantum feasibility metrics only within their valid scope.

5. What is the role of OR-Tools?
OR-Tools is the production solver for scalable, capacity-constrained multi-vehicle routing. It creates a reliable classical route seed and remains the operational fallback.

6. What is QAOA+ doing in the fleet scenario?
QAOA+ does not encode the entire fleet VRP. It evaluates a small, bounded neighbourhood of local moves around the OR-Tools route using a constraint-aware XY-mixer. This keeps the quantum experiment auditable and compatible with current NISQ limits.

7. Did you run on real quantum hardware?
We distinguish every backend explicitly. A record labelled "Local Statevector Simulator" is local simulation. A QUDORA record labelled Qamelion or QVLS-Q1 is a cloud emulator job. A claim of physical QPU execution requires a real IBM backend name and a real hardware job ID.

8. How can a judge verify a claimed quantum run?
The application stores the run ID, backend name, job ID, execution status, and QPU time when supplied. The Benchmark screen and persisted run history expose that evidence.

9. Why use QUDORA?
QUDORA provides an isolated cloud adapter for OpenQASM 2 circuit submission. It lets us execute the bounded QAOA+ experiment without mixing its SDK dependencies with the IBM Runtime environment.

10. What happens if the quantum provider is unavailable?
The operational OR-Tools route remains valid. QUASAR persists a disclosed quantum-evidence error instead of fabricating a result, so delivery operations are not blocked by a cloud queue or connection failure.

11. Are the fuel, cost, and time values measured savings?
No. They are explicitly labelled operational estimates based on stated assumptions: map-preview distance, 24 km/h, five minutes per stop, 9 L/100 km, and a fuel-price assumption. A savings claim requires a documented baseline.

12. Why can map distance differ from solver distance?
The optimizer records the distance matrix used for ranking. The map separately requests road geometry for display. When OSM road-graph retrieval fails safely, the solver uses a labelled Haversine fallback; the UI does not silently mix the two metrics.

13. What makes this production-oriented rather than only a research demo?
The system uses asynchronous background execution, request tracing, rate limiting, security headers, validated inputs, a durable SQLite run history, and an explicit classical fallback. The quantum path is pluggable rather than a single point of failure.

14. What is your business model?
The core model is SaaS: a subscription by fleet size or delivery-stop volume, usage credits for optimization runs, paid integrations with order-management or telematics systems, and an optional research tier for audited quantum benchmarking.

15. What is the next technical milestone?
Persist QUDORA/IBM job evidence for every provider run, collect repeated seeded benchmarks, compare QAOA+ feasibility against standard QAOA, and validate the bounded experiment on a physical QPU only when hardware job IDs and noise-aware results are available.
